Outreach Playbook

Who to contact, what to send, what to say when they push back, and what to do every week.

1. Ground rules

You are not selling a website. You are finding a business with a problem that costs it money, and introducing it to the people who fix that problem.

Five rules that keep you out of trouble and win more deals:

  1. Register before you contact. Always. That is what protects your 30%.
  2. Never quote a price. "Let me get you an exact figure from the team today" is a complete, professional answer.
  3. Ask more than you tell. You cannot qualify a lead you have not listened to.
  4. Follow up three times. Most partners lose deals at follow-up one. Deals are won at follow-up three.
  5. Log every conversation. If it is not in the tracker, it did not happen — and it cannot be defended if attribution is questioned.

2. Who to target

The best leads share four traits: they have a visible problem, they lose money by not fixing it, they can decide alone, and they can pay.

Highest-value, lowest-friction starts: businesses you already know, in segments you understand. Do not start with cold lists of strangers in industries you have never sold into.

Where to look. Your phone contacts. Your WhatsApp groups — university, family, neighbourhood, professional associations. Your Facebook and LinkedIn connections. Former employers and clients. Your gym, your mosque committee, your kids' school. The shops you visit every week. People you have already met will always beat cold outreach by a wide margin.

3. Your network map (do this on Day 1)

Write names against each bucket. Aim for 25 before you contact anyone.

People who owe me a favour

Then rank each name 1–3:

  • 3 — Hot. I know them well, they have a business, they have a visible problem.
  • 2 — Warm. I know them, they may have a business or know someone who does.
  • 1 — Cold. Contactable but no relationship.

Contact every 3 and 2 before you touch a single 1. This is the whole game.

5. Message templates

Adapt these. Copy-paste verbatim and people smell it instantly. Keep them short — WhatsApp is not email.

5.1 First touch — WhatsApp, English

Assalam-o-Alaikum [Name], hope you're well.

I've started working with Devzard — a software house that builds websites, online stores and custom systems for businesses.

I noticed [specific observation — "your menu isn't online", "your site doesn't load on mobile", "you're selling through DMs"]. That's usually costing more customers than it looks.

Worth a 10-minute chat? If there's nothing there, I'll say so and leave you alone.

5.2 First touch — WhatsApp, Roman Urdu

Assalam-o-Alaikum [Name], umeed hai aap theek hain.

Main Devzard ke saath kaam shuru kiya hai — yeh ek software house hai jo businesses ke liye websites, online stores aur custom systems banata hai.

Maine notice kiya ke [specific baat — "aapka menu online nahi hai", "site mobile pe theek nahi chalti"]. Aise cases mein customers zyada nuksan hote hain.

10 minute ki baat kar lein? Agar kuch kaam ki baat na nikle to main khud keh dunga aur aapko tang nahi karunga.

5.6 LinkedIn / Facebook DM

Hi [Name] — I work with Devzard, we build websites, online stores and custom software for growing businesses.

I came across [company] and noticed [specific observation]. We've solved this for businesses in [their industry].

If it's useful, I can share two examples of what that looks like — no pitch, just examples. Interested?

5.7 Cold email

Subject: [Company name] — [specific observation]

Hi [Name],

I work with Devzard, a software house in Pakistan that builds websites, e-commerce stores and custom systems for growing businesses.

I had a look at [company / page] and noticed [specific, factual observation — one line, no exaggeration].

We've helped similar businesses in [industry] with exactly that. If it's worth 10 minutes, I'll show you two examples and you can tell me whether it's relevant.

Regards,

[Your name] · Devzard Sales Partner

[Phone / WhatsApp]

Keep it under 120 words. One observation, one ask, one link at most. No attachments, no paragraphs.

5.8 Phone call script (first 30 seconds)

"Assalam-o-Alaikum, [Name]? Main [Your name] bol raha hoon, Devzard se.

Main aapko sirf ek minute lunga. Hum businesses ke liye websites aur online systems banate hain, aur maine notice kiya ke [specific observation].

Kya aap 2 minute de sakte hain, ya main baad mein call karun?"

Then stop talking and let them answer. If they say yes, move to the qualifying questions. If they say no, ask "Kis waqt call karun?" and write the time down.

6. Qualifying — the three questions that matter

Ask these early. If you cannot get clear answers, the lead is not real yet.

#Question (Roman Urdu)What you are actually learning
1"Abhi yeh kaam kaise chal raha hai?"Is there a real process today, and is it painful? No pain, no project
2"Agar yeh theek ho jaye to aapke liye iski kya value hai?"Is the problem big enough to pay for?
3"Iska faisla aap khud karenge ya kisi aur se poochna hoga?"Who signs? If it is not them, you need to meet the decision-maker

Must-know before booking a discovery call:

  • [ ] Business name and what they actually sell
  • [ ] Who decides, and their name and designation
  • [ ] The problem in their own words
  • [ ] Rough budget signal (even a range: "50k tak? 2 lakh tak?")
  • [ ] Timeline — when do they want it live
  • [ ] Named contact's phone, WhatsApp and email

Log all of it in the tracker, then message the team: "Lead ready — book discovery call."

7. Objection handling

Answer, then immediately ask a question, so you keep control of the conversation.

ObjectionResponse
"Bohat mehnga hai.""Samajh sakta hoon. Aapke zehen mein budget kitna hai? Team us range mein kya deliver kar sakti hai woh bata sakti hai — ho sakta hai poora plan na chahiye, sirf pehla step chahiye."
"Baad mein dekhenge.""Bilkul. Aap kis mahine mein karna chahenge? Main us waqt se pehle follow-up kar dunga, takay waqt pe kaam shuru ho sake." Then log the date and honour it.
"Hamara relative/friend bana deta hai.""Achha hai. Unse ek sawal: kya unke paas [specific thing — payment integration / SEO / mobile speed] ka experience hai? Main comparison nahi kar raha, bas ek 10-min call rakhein, aap khud decide karein."
"Pehle bhi paise lagaye, faida nahi hua.""Yeh sun kar bura laga. Exactly kya hua tha? … Kaam kya tha? … Theek hai — is baar hum [milestone-based, demo pehle, fixed scope] karte hain, iss liye aap poora paisa ek saath nahi lagayenge."
"Kya guarantee hai ke kaam aayega?""Website ka kaam guaranteed hai — scope, timeline, delivery, sab likha hua. Sales ka result koi guarantee nahi kar sakta, aur jo karta hai woh sach nahi bol raha. Hum pehle ek chhota phase karenge, aap result dekh kar aage barhein."
"Team ko baat karni hai / sochna hai.""Zaroor. Aap kis se baat karenge? Main unko sirf technical sawalon ke liye call pe bula sakta hoon — usually wohi confusion khatam kar deta hai."
"Believe nahi hota ke aap kar sakte hain.""Bilkul jaiz baat hai. Main aapko do live examples bhejta hoon jo humne banaye — aur agar chahein to ek free mock-up bhi bana denge, paise se pehle."
"Hamara business chhota hai, humein nahi chahiye.""Aapka kitna turnover hai? … Iss size pe usually log [specific small thing] se shuru karte hain. Poori website zaroori nahi — ek page bhi kaam kar deta hai."
"Aap Devzard mein kaam karte hain?""Nahi — main sales partner hoon. Technical team Devzard ki hai, aur wahi delivery karegi. Main aapka point of contact rahunga."
"Aap kis kesy ho? Main aapko nahi janta.""[Mutual contact] ne refer kiya" — or — "[How you know them]. Main aapko number de raha hoon, aap team se direct baat kar sakte hain."

Two things never to do under pressure: invent a price, or invent a capability. "Main confirm karke aaj shaam tak bata deta hoon" costs you nothing and buys you credibility.

The team takes it from there: discovery call, scope, proposal within 48 hours. Your job is to be on that call and to keep the client responding afterwards.

9. Post-proposal follow-up sequence

After the proposal is sent, run this. Do not deviate, do not skip steps.

WhenMessage
Day 1"Assalam-o-Alaikum [Name], proposal bhej diya hai. Ek nazar daal lein — koi bhi cheez clear nahi ho to main samjha dunga. Koi jaldi nahi, bas confirm kar dena ke mil gaya."
Day 3"Proposal pe koi sawal? Scope ya price pe kuch adjust karna hai to bataein — team dekh sakti hai."
Day 7"Sirf follow-up: aapka timeline kya hai? Agar iss mahine shuru karna hai to hum slot rakh lein, warna main [next month] pe baat karun?"
Day 14"Kya decision hua? Agar abhi nahi, koi masla nahi — main [date] pe dubara check kar lunga. Agar haan, aapko advance aur timeline bhej dete hain."
Day 30"Kuch arsa ho gaya — bas yeh dekhne ke liye ke plan badla ya wahi hai. Naya kuch hai to bataein."

Every follow-up ends with a question or a date. "Just following up" is not a message — it is noise.

When to walk away: if the answer to three consecutive follow-ups is silence or a clear no, mark the lead Nurture and come back in 90 days with something new — a relevant example, a new offer, a price change.

10. Referral and re-sell

The cheapest deal you will ever close is with a client you already have.

Ask for a referral immediately after a win:

"[Name], project live ho gaya — achha laga aap ke saath kaam karke. Ek chhoti si request: aapke janne mein koi 2 log hain jinko is tarah ka kaam chahiye ho? Sirf naam aur number — main baaki khud kar lunga."

Ask 30 days after delivery, when the value is visible:

"[Name], website chal rahi hai theek? Koi bhi issue ho to bataein. Ek aur cheez — kya [upsell: online payments / SEO / maintenance] ka plan hai? Bahut clients iss step se shuru karte hain."

Retainers are the real prize. A maintenance or hosting retainer pays you every month for as long as the client stays. Two or three retainers change the economics of your whole month — see Example 5 in the Commission Guide.

Fixed rhythms

  • Monday (15 min): review the tracker. Every lead must have a next action and a date. No exceptions.
  • Wednesday (15 min): team check-in. What is hot, what is stuck, what needs a proposal.
  • Friday (15 min): week in numbers. Sent, replied, calls booked, proposals, closed.

If the numbers are weak, the problem is almost always one of three things: the wrong segment (no real pain), the wrong message (you talked about us, not about them), or too few contacts (the most common cause by far).

13. Do and do not

Do

  • Register the lead first, every single time.
  • Reference something specific and true about their business.
  • Ask three questions before you mention a price.
  • Get a date on every follow-up.
  • Message the team the moment a lead is ready — speed is your advantage.
  • Say "I'll confirm and come back today" and then actually come back today.
  • Log the conversation in the tracker the same day.

Do not

  • Quote a price, a discount or a delivery date you have not been given in writing.
  • Claim a client, a result or a capability Devzard does not have.
  • Send the same message to 50 people in one blast.
  • Contact anyone on the excluded list.
  • Promise a guarantee about traffic, ranking or revenue.
  • Keep a lead in your head instead of in the tracker — it is worth 30% of the project.
  • Give up after one follow-up. The deal is usually at number three.
